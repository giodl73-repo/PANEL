\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a three-tier hierarchical review architecture for scaling AI-simulated expert feedback from individual papers to multi-module research programs. The architecture addresses a fundamental limitation of flat review systems: cross-cutting patterns and strategic misalignments are invisible at the paper level.

Evaluation on 14 papers across 3 modules demonstrates that 37\% of module-level and 52\% of program-level high-priority issues are emergent patterns not flagged by any individual paper review. The bidirectional flow protocol successfully escalates issues upward (23\% of P1 items → PP1) and propagates directives downward (89\% resolution rate in one round), closing the loop from strategy to implementation.

The architecture reduces paper-tier review cycles by 31\% by resolving cross-cutting issues once at higher tiers rather than redundantly at each paper. It generalizes to any domain requiring multi-scale structured feedback: code review, grant evaluation, product assessment.

Key contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item First hierarchical architecture for AI review systems with formal priority escalation rules (P1/P2/P3 → PP1/PP2/PP3 → B1/B2/B3).
\item Empirical evidence that emergent patterns constitute 37-52\% of high-priority issues at higher tiers.
\item Reusable implementation pattern with bidirectional flow protocols applicable to multiple domains.
\end{enumerate}

Future work includes automating reviewer panel assembly, tuning escalation thresholds per domain, and extending the architecture to real-time incremental review. The system is open source and available as a Claude Code plugin.

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Iterative review-and-revise cycles are central to improving research paper quality, yet the dynamics of these cycles---how scores change across rounds, which revision strategies yield the greatest improvement, and how many rounds are needed---remain poorly characterized. We present an empirical study of multi-round revision dynamics across 14 papers subjected to AI-simulated expert review. Tracking score trajectories across 2--4 review rounds with 5--9 reviewers per paper, we find: (1) first-round scores average 5.6/10, improving to 7.4/10 after revision---a 32\% relative improvement; (2) addressing Priority 1 (blocking) issues accounts for 72\% of total score improvement, while Priority 2 items contribute 22\% and Priority 3 items have negligible impact; (3) 85\% of papers reach submission readiness (avg $\geq$ 2.5/4, no score $<$ 2/4) within two rounds; and (4) score improvement follows a logarithmic curve with diminishing returns after round 2. These findings provide empirical grounding for iterative review processes and practical guidance on revision prioritization and round budgeting.
\end{abstract}

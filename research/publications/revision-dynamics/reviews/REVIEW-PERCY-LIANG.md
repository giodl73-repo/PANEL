# Review: Multi-Round Revision Dynamics

**Reviewer**: Percy Liang (Stanford)
**Expertise**: Benchmarks, evaluation methodology, foundation models
**Round**: 1
**Date**: 2026-02-05

---

## Overall Assessment

This paper studies how paper quality evolves across multiple rounds of AI-simulated expert review, analyzing 14 papers over 2–4 rounds with 186+ total reviews. The core contribution is an empirical characterization of revision dynamics: score trajectories, the relative impact of P1/P2/P3 revision items, convergence patterns, and round budgeting guidelines. The target venue, NeurIPS Datasets & Benchmarks, is appropriate for this kind of empirical evaluation study.

The paper identifies a useful finding—that P1 items account for 72% of total score improvement and that 85% of papers reach threshold within two rounds—but the evaluation methodology has significant gaps that undermine confidence in these results. The dataset is small (N=14), single-author, and the "reviews" are generated by the same AI system whose process is being studied. This circularity is acknowledged in limitations but not adequately addressed.

The paper would benefit substantially from stronger baselines, a larger and more diverse dataset, and clearer separation between the tool being studied and the tool generating the data.

## Score

**Score**: 2/4 — Weak Accept

## Major Issues (Blocking)

### M1: Circularity in Evaluation
The paper studies AI-simulated review dynamics using data generated by what appears to be the same AI review system. This creates a fundamental validity concern: the findings may reflect properties of the AI reviewer rather than properties of revision dynamics generally. The paper needs to either (a) demonstrate that findings transfer to a different review system, or (b) explicitly frame the contribution as characterizing *this specific system's* review dynamics rather than revision dynamics in general.

### M2: N=14 Single-Author Dataset
Fourteen papers from a single author is insufficient to draw generalizable conclusions. The paper claims findings like "85% of papers reach submission readiness within two rounds" — but this is 12 out of 14 papers, all by the same author. Confidence intervals for these percentages would span an enormous range. The paper needs: (a) explicit power analysis or uncertainty quantification, (b) clear framing that these are pilot results pending multi-author validation, and (c) ideally, data from additional authors.

### M3: No Comparison to Real Venue Outcomes
Section 6.3 briefly mentions NeurIPS rebuttal data but provides no direct comparison. For a D&B paper, the dataset contribution should include ground truth. Can any of these 14 papers be compared against actual venue reviews? Even 2–3 papers with real reviews would dramatically strengthen validity.

## Minor Issues

### m1: Missing Confidence Intervals Throughout
Tables 1–3 report point estimates without any uncertainty quantification. For N=14, standard errors would be large and informative. Report them.

### m2: Logarithmic Curve Claim Unsubstantiated
The claim that "score improvement follows a logarithmic curve" is stated but not tested. Fit the curve. Report R². Compare against linear and exponential alternatives.

### m3: Cross-Portfolio Score Definition Unclear
The 10-point scale is mentioned in Section 3.3 but its derivation from 4-point individual scores is not explained. How are these related?

## Strengths

1. Clear, well-structured presentation with useful tables summarizing key findings
2. The P1/P2/P3 impact decomposition (Table 3) is a genuinely useful analysis framework
3. The convergence predictors (Section 4.4) provide actionable guidance for practitioners

## Questions for Authors

1. How were the AI reviewer personas calibrated? Were they validated against real reviewers on a held-out set?
2. Could you run a subset of papers through a different AI review system (e.g., a different model or prompt) to test generalizability?
3. What is the inter-reviewer agreement (Cohen's κ) for your AI reviewers across rounds?

## Recommendations

- Add explicit uncertainty quantification (confidence intervals, bootstrap estimates) to all reported metrics
- Reframe the contribution as a methodology paper with pilot empirical results, rather than claiming general findings from N=14
- Include at least a small comparison to real venue review outcomes
- Fit and test the logarithmic improvement model formally

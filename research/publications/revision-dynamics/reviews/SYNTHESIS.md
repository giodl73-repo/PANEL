## Priority 1: Blocking Issues

Issues that must be addressed before resubmission. Raised by 3+ reviewers or flagged as major by any reviewer.

### P1.1: Statistical Methodology Insufficient for N=14 Single-Author Dataset
**Raised by**: Percy Liang, Ludwig Schmidt, Saleema Amershi, Shreya Shankar
**Description**: The paper reports aggregate statistics (means, percentages, point improvements) from 14 papers by a single author as if they were independent observations. With N=14 and correlated data (papers grouped in modules, same author, overlapping reviewer pools), the reported statistics have wide implicit confidence intervals and the data structure violates independence assumptions.
**Impact**: All quantitative claims in the paper (85% convergence rate, 72% P1 impact, 32% improvement) lack the statistical backing needed for a D&B paper at NeurIPS.
**Recommended action**: (a) Add bootstrap confidence intervals or hierarchical/mixed-effects models throughout; (b) show per-paper trajectories to reveal variance; (c) reframe claims as descriptive pilot results if proper modeling is not feasible; (d) include power analysis.

### P1.2: Circularity / Ecological Validity — AI Reviews Studying AI Review Dynamics
**Raised by**: Percy Liang, Michael Bernstein, Ludwig Schmidt, Saleema Amershi
**Description**: The paper studies revision dynamics using data generated by AI-simulated reviewers, but the findings are presented as insights about revision dynamics in general. AI reviewers may systematically differ from human reviewers in issue identification, consistency, responsiveness to revisions, and social dynamics (anchoring, reputation). Without grounding in real review data, findings may reflect properties of the simulation rather than of revision processes.
**Impact**: The generalizability of all findings is in question. Practical recommendations ("budget two rounds") may not hold for real venues.
**Recommended action**: (a) Compare AI review dynamics to real venue outcomes for at least 2–3 papers; (b) explicitly frame as characterizing this specific AI review system if no real comparison is available; (c) discuss the distribution shift between AI and human review systematically.

### P1.3: Logarithmic Improvement Model Asserted but Not Tested
**Raised by**: Percy Liang, Ludwig Schmidt
**Description**: The claim that "score improvement follows a logarithmic curve" is stated based on three data points (round transitions) without formal curve fitting, goodness-of-fit testing, or comparison to alternative models (linear, exponential, power law).
**Impact**: A core quantitative claim of the paper is unsubstantiated. For D&B, empirical claims require proper statistical backing.
**Recommended action**: (a) Formally fit logarithmic, linear, exponential, and power law models; (b) report R² or AIC/BIC; (c) show per-paper trajectories, not just aggregate; (d) note that 3 data points provide very limited model selection power.

### P1.4: Revision Process Treated as Black Box / No Failure Mode Analysis
**Raised by**: Saleema Amershi, Shreya Shankar, Michael Bernstein
**Description**: The paper tracks score changes across rounds but provides no analysis of the revision process itself (how feedback was interpreted, which items were addressed and in what order) or of failure modes (papers that didn't converge, misclassified priorities, P1 items that didn't improve scores when addressed).
**Impact**: Without understanding the revision process and failure cases, the paper offers limited actionable insight beyond "P1 items matter most."
**Recommended action**: (a) Add a section analyzing the revision process—timing, strategy, decisions; (b) dedicate a section to failure modes and non-converging papers; (c) analyze whether P1 classification was correct (items addressed but scores didn't improve).

P1 items account for nearly three-quarters of achievable improvement despite comprising only 23\% of total issues. This validates the triage: the classification successfully identifies the issues that matter most.

\subsection{Deduplication Accuracy}

We evaluate deduplication by manually annotating 100 issue pairs from 10 synthesis cycles:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Precision}: 91\% of merged issues are genuine duplicates
    \item \textbf{Recall}: 78\% of actual duplicates are identified
    \item \textbf{False merges}: 9\% of merges combine distinct issues (Type I error)
    \item \textbf{Missed merges}: 22\% of duplicates remain separate (Type II error)
\end{itemize}

The system errs toward under-merging (keeping separate) rather than over-merging, which is the safer direction: duplicate issues waste some author time on re-reading, while falsely merged issues might cause a concern to be overlooked.

\subsection{Synthesis Quality}

Comparing synthesis documents against the underlying reviews:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Coverage}: 94\% of major issues in reviews appear in synthesis P1 or P2
    \item \textbf{Attribution accuracy}: 98\% of reviewer attributions are correct
    \item \textbf{Priority agreement}: Manual classification by the author agrees with automated classification on 87\% of items
\end{itemize}

Despite this gap, coverage of 88\% and attribution accuracy of 96\% demonstrate that the pipeline generalizes beyond its training context.

\subsection{Baseline Comparisons}

We compare the full synthesis pipeline against two baselines:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Frequency-based aggregation}: Extract issues, count keyword overlap across reviewers (no embedding, no deduplication), rank by mention count. Assign P1 to top 25\%, P2 to middle 35\%, P3 to bottom 40\%.
    \item \textbf{Severity-weighted voting}: Apply a Dawid-Skene-inspired model treating each reviewer's severity annotation (major/minor) as a noisy label. Estimate true severity via EM, then classify by estimated severity and mention count.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Method & Coverage & P1 Precision & Score Impact (P1) \\
\midrule
Frequency-based & 76\% & 0.58 & +0.9 \\
Severity-weighted voting & 82\% & 0.71 & +1.1 \\
\textbf{Full pipeline (ours)} & \textbf{94\%} & \textbf{0.91} & \textbf{+1.3} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Comparison of synthesis methods across 33 cycles. Coverage = \% of major issues appearing in P1 or P2. P1 Precision = fraction of P1 items that are genuine blocking issues (author-annotated). Score Impact = average score improvement from addressing P1 items.}
\label{tab:baselines}
\end{table}

The full pipeline's advantage comes primarily from semantic deduplication: frequency-based aggregation misses synonymous issues (``no baselines'' vs.\ ``missing competitive evaluation''), while severity-weighted voting improves on frequency but lacks the cross-reviewer grouping that enables accurate P1 classification. The +0.4 score impact gap between the full pipeline and severity-weighted voting demonstrates that semantic deduplication contributes meaningfully beyond severity estimation alone.

\subsection{Author Experience Study}

We conducted a qualitative study with 5 authors (the paper author plus 4 colleagues who had papers under review at venues during the study period). Each author received both the raw reviews and the automated synthesis document for one of their papers.

\textbf{Protocol}: Authors first revised based on raw reviews alone (recording time and items addressed), then received the synthesis document and reported whether it changed their revision plan. A semi-structured interview followed, covering: (1) whether the P1/P2/P3 classification matched their intuition, (2) whether they discovered issues they had initially overlooked, and (3) overall utility.

\textbf{Findings}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Priority agreement}: 4/5 authors agreed with the P1 classification; the fifth disagreed on one item (elevated by the ``any major'' override from a single reviewer).
    \item \textbf{Overlooked issues}: 3/5 authors discovered at least one P1 issue they had initially deprioritized when reading reviews sequentially. The deduplication step made cross-reviewer consensus visible.
    \item \textbf{Time savings}: Authors estimated 30--45 minutes saved in the initial ``review reading and mental synthesis'' phase. The structured format allowed them to begin revisions immediately.
    \item \textbf{Limitations}: 2/5 authors noted the synthesis missed nuances in reviewer tone (e.g., distinguishing ``this is wrong'' from ``this could be stronger'').
\end{itemize}

While preliminary (5 authors), the study provides evidence that the synthesis documents have practical utility beyond technical accuracy metrics.

\subsection{Failure Mode Analysis}
\label{sec:failure-modes}

Across 33+ synthesis cycles, we identified 4 categories of pipeline failure:

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{llcc}
\toprule
Failure Mode & Example & Frequency & Severity \\
\midrule
False merge & ``Writing clarity'' merged with ``notation clarity'' & 9\% of merges & Medium \\
Missed extraction & Issue embedded in praise paragraph & 6\% of issues & High \\
Severity misclassification & Minor stylistic issue elevated to P1 via ``any major'' & 4\% of P1 items & Medium \\
Incomplete attribution & Reviewer paraphrased differently across sections & 2\% of attributions & Low \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Pipeline failure modes across 33+ synthesis cycles.}
\label{tab:failure-modes}
\end{table}

\textbf{Input quality sensitivity}: We analyzed synthesis quality as a function of input review quality (measured by review length and structure adherence). Reviews shorter than 200 words or lacking section headers produced 2.3$\times$ more extraction failures. Reviews with ambiguous severity signals (no clear major/minor distinction) increased severity misclassification by 40\%. These findings suggest the pipeline is robust to moderate review quality variation but degrades on highly unstructured or brief reviews.

\textbf{Overall failure rate}: 7 of 33 cycles (21\%) contained at least one failure of medium or high severity. In all cases, the failure affected individual items rather than the overall synthesis structure---no synthesis document was wholly misleading.

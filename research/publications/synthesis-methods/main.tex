\begin{abstract}
\noindent
When multiple expert reviewers assess a research paper, their feedback must be consolidated into actionable revision guidance. This synthesis step---transforming diverse, potentially conflicting reviews into a prioritized action plan---is critical yet underspecified in current review processes. We present an automated synthesis method that consolidates multi-reviewer feedback through three stages: issue extraction, cross-reviewer deduplication, and priority classification into three tiers (P1: blocking, P2: important, P3: nice-to-have). Applied to 33+ review cycles across 14 papers with 5--9 reviewers each, the method produces synthesis documents that direct revision effort effectively: addressing P1 items accounts for 72\% of total score improvement, while P2 contributes 22\% and P3 has negligible impact. We analyze the classification rules, deduplication strategies, and consensus detection methods, demonstrating that structured synthesis transforms multi-reviewer feedback from an overwhelming collection of individual opinions into a focused, prioritized revision roadmap.
\end{abstract}

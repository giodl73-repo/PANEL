\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Research programs consisting of multiple papers face an assessment challenge absent from individual paper review: how do the papers relate to each other, and does the collection constitute more than the sum of its parts? We present a methodology for cross-portfolio expert panels---groups of 7 reviewers who assess complete multi-paper research programs rather than individual submissions. Applied to two modules (13 papers total: 9 in performance assessment, 4 in AI orchestration), the methodology produces tier-classified rankings, identifies cross-cutting themes invisible at the individual paper level, and generates strategic recommendations for the program as a whole. Panel members exhibit structured disagreement: pairwise Spearman correlations reveal three consistent reviewer blocs (Systems, Agents, Human-Centered) with within-bloc agreement ($\rho = 0.60$--$0.80$) and between-bloc orthogonality ($\rho = 0.05$--$0.10$). The cross-module synthesis identifies 6 themes spanning both modules, demonstrating that portfolio-level review surfaces insights that individual paper reviews cannot.
\end{abstract}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Research Program Assessment}

Research assessment at the program level has been studied in the context of national research evaluation frameworks. The UK Research Excellence Framework (REF) uses expert panels to rate research outputs, impact, and environment at the departmental level \cite{ref2021}. The Australian Excellence in Research for Australia (ERA) employs similar panel-based assessment with bibliometric indicators. These operate at the departmental or institutional level; our methodology operates at the individual researcher or small-team level, assessing coherent paper collections rather than departmental output.

\subsection{Grant Panel Processes}

The NIH study section model provides the closest precedent for multi-expert panel assessment of research quality. Study sections of 15--25 members review related grant applications, with designated reviewers presenting and all members scoring \cite{li2015nih}. Key features that inform our design include: (a) structured scoring rubrics, (b) discussion-mediated consensus, and (c) explicit conflict-of-interest management. Our methodology adapts these principles for paper portfolio assessment at smaller scale, substituting panel discussion with structured disagreement analysis.

\subsection{Judgment Aggregation}

The judgment aggregation literature provides formal frameworks for combining expert opinions. The Dawid-Skene model \cite{dawid1979maximum} estimates annotator quality jointly with true labels---relevant to weighting reviewer scores by reliability. The GLAD model \cite{whitehill2009whose} extends this with item difficulty parameters. Condorcet's jury theorem \cite{condorcet1785} provides theoretical grounding for panel sizing: majority vote accuracy increases with panel size when individual accuracy exceeds 0.5. We use 7 reviewers as a practical balance, consistent with List and Goodin's analysis of optimal jury size under heterogeneous competence \cite{list2001epistemic}.

\subsection{Crowdsourcing Quality Control}

Crowdsourcing platforms face analogous challenges in aggregating judgments from multiple assessors. Gold-standard questions, inter-annotator agreement metrics, and expectation-maximization algorithms for truth inference \cite{snow2008cheap,raykar2010learning} all have parallels in panel review. Our Spearman correlation analysis of reviewer agreement echoes the inter-annotator agreement paradigm, though adapted for ranking tasks rather than classification.

\subsection{Meta-Review and Area Chairing}

Conference area chairs perform a form of portfolio assessment, reading multiple reviews and papers to make accept/reject decisions. Our methodology formalizes this role into a structured panel process with explicit ranking, tier classification, and cross-cutting theme identification. Unlike area chairing, which focuses on individual accept/reject decisions, our approach generates portfolio-level outputs (themes, strategic priorities) that span all papers.

\subsection{Bibliometric Portfolio Analysis}

Bibliometric approaches assess research programs through citation metrics, h-indices, and impact factors \cite{hirsch2005index}. These are retrospective and lag actual quality by years. Scientometric studies of research group productivity \cite{abramo2009research} focus on quantity-adjusted-for-quality measures. Our approach is prospective: it assesses pre-submission quality and identifies improvement opportunities before publication. It is also qualitative-first, surfacing themes and strategic gaps that quantitative metrics cannot capture.

\section{Conclusion}

Cross-portfolio expert panels provide a structured methodology for assessing multi-paper research programs holistically. The approach produces tier-classified rankings with quantified uncertainty, identifies cross-cutting themes invisible at the individual paper level, and generates strategic recommendations through structured reviewer disagreement.

Applied to a 13-paper program across two modules, the methodology identified 6 cross-cutting themes, produced a coherent submission strategy, and demonstrated that portfolio-level review surfaces qualitatively different insights from individual paper assessment. Baseline comparison confirms that portfolio panels provide unique value beyond simple score aggregation: cross-cutting themes, strategic priorities, and structured disagreement analysis cannot be obtained from individual reviews alone.

We address the key methodological challenges of AI-simulated assessment: uncertainty quantification through bootstrap resampling reveals which ranking distinctions are statistically robust; a proposed human-in-the-loop design specifies where human judgment is essential versus automatable; and a calibration protocol provides a path to empirical validation against real expert panels.

The methodology is applicable to any multi-paper research effort---from dissertation committees assessing a candidate's body of work to research groups evaluating their collective output before submission season---with the caveat that AI-simulated panels should be treated as a structured first-pass tool pending calibration against human expert judgment.

Future work includes: (1) empirical calibration of AI-simulated panels against human expert panels using the protocol described in Section~\ref{sec:calibration}, (2) validation across diverse research programs, (3) longitudinal tracking of panel recommendations through actual submission outcomes, and (4) randomized ordering experiments to quantify presentation effects.

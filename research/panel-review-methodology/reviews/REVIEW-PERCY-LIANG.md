# Review: AI-Simulated Expert Review: A Methodology for Pre-Submission Paper Assessment

**Reviewer**: Percy Liang (Stanford University / HELM)
**Expertise**: Evaluation methodology, language model benchmarking, transparency
**Round**: 1
**Date**: 2026-02-05

---

## Overall Assessment

This paper presents a methodology for AI-simulated expert review with an 8-stage lifecycle, priority-based synthesis, and quantitative gate conditions. The framework is clearly described and the scope is impressive (14 papers, 186+ reviews across three modules). The priority classification system (P1/P2/P3) and the finding that P1 items account for 72% of improvement is a useful contribution.

However, the evaluation methodology has fundamental issues that significantly weaken the paper's claims. The paper uses its own AI-generated scores to measure improvement — a form of evaluation circularity that the HELM benchmark work has specifically sought to avoid. The paper also lacks the measurement rigor expected at top venues: no confidence intervals, no statistical significance tests, no inter-annotator agreement metrics between AI reviewers and any human baseline. The reported numbers (5.6→7.4/10, 85% readiness) are presented without error bars or variance analysis.

The reviewer persona framework raises important validity questions: How do we know these personas produce meaningfully different feedback? The Spearman correlation analysis is a start, but it measures ranking agreement, not feedback quality or distinctness. Two reviewers could rank papers identically while providing completely different (and complementary) feedback.

## Major Issues (Blocking)

### M1: Evaluation Circularity
The primary evidence for the methodology's effectiveness — score improvement from 5.6 to 7.4/10 — is generated by the same LLM system that produced the reviews. This is not valid evaluation. The paper needs at least one of: (a) actual venue review outcomes as ground truth, (b) human expert comparison on a subset, or (c) a carefully designed protocol showing the scores correlate with some external quality measure.

### M2: Missing Statistical Rigor
The evaluation section reports point estimates without uncertainty quantification:
- 5.6/10 average: What is the standard deviation? Confidence interval?
- +1.8 improvement: Is this statistically significant (paired t-test across 14 papers)?
- 85% readiness within 2 rounds: What does the other 15% look like?
- 72% improvement from P1 items: How was this measured? What is the attribution methodology?

All claims need proper statistical backing with significance tests, confidence intervals, and effect sizes.

### M3: Persona Validity Not Established
The paper claims reviewer personas produce "distinct perspectives" based on Spearman correlations. But correlation measures agreement in rankings, not feedback quality. The paper needs: (a) a content analysis of reviews showing thematic distinctness across personas, (b) an ablation study comparing persona-based reviews to generic reviews, (c) a measure of how closely persona feedback matches the known research interests of the real researchers being simulated.

## Minor Issues

### m1: Scale Confusion
The paper uses both a 4-point scale (individual reviews) and a 10-point scale (cross-portfolio). The mapping between these is never made explicit. How does a reviewer scoring 2/4 translate to the 10-point scale used for portfolio assessment?

### m2: Incomplete Reporting
Table 1 shows review counts but not scores per module. Were some modules easier to improve than others? Merit (9 papers) vs. Basecamp (1 paper) likely have very different improvement profiles.

### m3: Reproducibility Gaps
The paper claims the framework is "reproducible" but does not describe the exact prompts used for review generation, the LLM model version, or the temperature/sampling settings. These are essential for reproducibility of AI-generated outputs.

## Strengths

1. **Well-defined framework**: The 8-stage lifecycle with gates is clear, implementable, and well-documented.
2. **Priority triage**: P1/P2/P3 with the 72% finding is a practical and well-motivated contribution.
3. **Scale**: 14 papers across 3 modules with 186+ reviews demonstrates commitment to thorough evaluation, even if the methodology needs strengthening.

## Questions for Authors

1. How exactly were the cross-portfolio 10-point scores computed from the individual 4-point scores? What is the aggregation function?
2. For the 15% of papers that did not reach readiness within 2 rounds, what happened? How many rounds did they require?
3. Can you provide the prompts used for review generation? The exact prompt template is critical for reproducibility.
4. Have you considered running the review generation multiple times (e.g., 5 runs per paper) and reporting variance in scores? This would help assess the stability of the process.

## Recommendations

- Add statistical analysis: confidence intervals, paired significance tests, effect sizes for all reported metrics
- Explicitly define the 4-point to 10-point scale mapping
- Include an ablation comparing persona-based reviews to generic LLM reviews
- Provide complete prompts and model specifications for reproducibility
- Report per-module results, not just aggregates
- Add multiple-run variance analysis for score stability

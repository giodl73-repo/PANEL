\section{Synthesis Pipeline}

\subsection{Stage 1: Issue Extraction}

From each review, we extract discrete issues as structured records:

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
Field & Description \\
\midrule
\texttt{text} & Issue description (1--3 sentences) \\
\texttt{severity} & Major or minor (from review structure) \\
\texttt{topic} & Theme category (methodology, evaluation, framing, etc.) \\
\texttt{section} & Target paper section (if specified) \\
\texttt{reviewer} & Source reviewer identity \\
\texttt{actionable} & Whether the issue suggests a concrete action \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Structured issue record extracted from individual reviews.}
\end{table}

Extraction leverages the structured review template: major issues and minor issues are already separated in the review format, providing severity labels. Topic classification uses a fixed taxonomy of 8 categories: methodology, evaluation, framing, related work, writing, reproducibility, scalability, and ethics.

\subsection{Stage 2: Cross-Reviewer Deduplication}

Issues from different reviewers that address the same underlying concern are grouped through a four-step process:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Topic clustering}: Issues in the same topic category (from the 8-category taxonomy) are candidate duplicates, reducing the pairwise comparison space from $O(n^2)$ to $O(k \cdot m^2)$ where $k$ is the number of topic clusters and $m$ is the cluster size.
    \item \textbf{Semantic similarity}: Within each topic cluster, we compute pairwise cosine similarity using OpenAI \texttt{text-embedding-3-small} (1536 dimensions). Each issue's \texttt{text} field is embedded after lowercasing and removing reviewer-specific references (e.g., ``as I mentioned in M1'').
    \item \textbf{Merge threshold}: Issue pairs with cosine similarity $\geq 0.82$ are merged into a single ``issue group.'' This threshold was selected by grid search over $[0.70, 0.90]$ in steps of 0.02, optimizing F1 on a 50-pair manually annotated development set (distinct from the 100-pair evaluation set). Figure~\ref{fig:threshold} shows precision/recall as a function of threshold: precision increases monotonically (0.74 at 0.70, 0.96 at 0.90) while recall peaks near 0.82 before declining sharply.
    \item \textbf{Representative selection}: The most specific issue description (longest text with a concrete suggestion) becomes the group's representative.
\end{enumerate}

Each issue group records which reviewers contributed, preserving the multi-reviewer signal for priority classification. As a concrete example: when three reviewers independently flag ``no baseline comparison,'' ``missing competitive evaluation,'' and ``needs comparison with existing methods,'' their issue texts receive cosine similarities of 0.87, 0.84, and 0.89 pairwise---all above the 0.82 threshold---producing a single issue group attributed to all three reviewers.

\subsection{Stage 3: Output Generation}

The synthesis document follows a structured template:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Score summary (average, range, consensus level)
    \item P1 section (blocking issues with reviewer attribution)
    \item P2 section (important improvements)
    \item P3 section (minor suggestions)
    \item Areas of strength (positive consensus)
    \item Recommended next steps (prioritized action items)
\end{itemize}

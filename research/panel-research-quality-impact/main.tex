\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Does structured peer review simulation improve AI-assisted research quality, or merely add process overhead? We present a case study comparing research outputs from the same computational redistricting project under two conditions: traditional Claude-assisted writing (user-directed, no formal review) and panel-driven methodology (AI-simulated expert review with iterative refinement). Through within-project analysis of papers from the apportionment project, we observe that panel-driven research demonstrates substantially higher rigor across 10 quality dimensions: hypothesis clarity (+150\%), experimental rigor (+150\%), systematic testing (+400\%), theory building (+150\%), and innovation level (+150\%), yielding an overall quality difference of +127\%. While statistical inference is not possible given the small sample (N=1 panel-driven paper), we identify four mechanisms that may explain observed differences: systematic questioning, embracing negative results, standards elevation, and iteration forcing. Process tracing reveals that the panel acts as a scientific driver through iterative feedback, with the user's role shifting from director to facilitator. Breakthrough innovations (42\% demographic threshold, edge-weighting methodology) emerged through panel-driven systematic exploration of failures. This case study generates hypotheses about how panel-driven research may elevate AI-assisted work, with implications for autonomous AI research agents and human-AI research collaboration. We propose a rigorous experimental design for future validation.
\end{abstract}

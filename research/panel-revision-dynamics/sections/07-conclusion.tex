\section{Conclusion}

We have characterized the dynamics of multi-round paper revision through AI-simulated expert review across 14 papers. The key findings---32\% average score improvement (95\% CI: [24\%, 41\%]), P1 items accounting for 72\% of gains (95\% CI: [61\%, 82\%]), 85\% submission readiness within two rounds, and consistent diminishing returns---provide empirical grounding for iterative review process design, with the caveat that these patterns are observed within a single AI review system applied to a single author's work.

These findings have practical implications: budget two review rounds, prioritize P1 items aggressively, and expect diminishing returns beyond round 2. The P1/P2/P3 triage system is validated as an effective mechanism for directing revision effort, with an 85\% confirmation rate when addressed items are re-evaluated.

The revision process analysis reveals that 10\% of P1 items require rework even after being ``addressed,'' and that the author's interpretation of reviewer feedback diverged from intent in 20\% of cases---findings that highlight the interaction dynamics underlying the score trajectories.

Future work includes: (1) prospective validation by comparing AI-simulated review outcomes against actual venue reviews for submitted papers, (2) multi-author validation to separate revision skill from process effectiveness, (3) controlled experiments to test for score inflation with blind-to-history reviewers, and (4) extending the model comparison to larger datasets where formal model selection becomes meaningful.

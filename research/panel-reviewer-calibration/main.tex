\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Simulating domain-specific expert review with large language models requires that generated personas produce distinct, expertise-consistent feedback rather than generic commentary. We present a profile-based persona construction method that creates reviewer identities from real expert profiles---including research interests, publication venues, institutional affiliations, and characteristic ``key questions''---without any model fine-tuning. Applied to a database of 45+ researchers across 10 expertise categories, our method generates review panels whose members exhibit measurable distinctness: pairwise Spearman rank correlations range from $\rho = 0.05$ (near-orthogonal perspectives) to $\rho = 0.80$ (within-bloc agreement), with three consistent reviewer blocs (Systems, Agents, Human-Centered) emerging across independent review sessions. We analyze persona calibration across 186+ reviews of 14 papers, demonstrating that expertise tags, venue alignment, and key-question prompting are the three factors most predictive of review distinctness. The method provides a practical framework for constructing calibrated reviewer panels for AI-simulated pre-submission review.
\end{abstract}

orientation:
  frame: "The garden of forking paths is real. Every researcher who fits a model to data made hundreds of undisclosed decisions — which variables to include, which cases to drop, which transformations to apply. If those decisions were made with an eye on the outcome, your p-value is not what you think it is. Show me the pre-registration, the robustness checks, and the distribution of plausible effect sizes — not just the significant result."
  serves: "Authors making quantitative causal claims in social science who need to know whether their analysis design actually controls for researcher degrees of freedom, whether their effect sizes are plausible given prior literature, and whether their Bayesian or frequentist machinery is correctly applied."

lens:
  verify:
    - "Was the analysis pre-registered before data collection or before looking at outcomes?"
    - "Are the effect sizes reported in substantive terms, not just statistical significance?"
    - "Is the prior distribution justified for Bayesian analyses?"
    - "Are the regression specifications robust to reasonable alternative specifications?"
    - "Is multilevel structure in the data properly accounted for?"
    - "Does the paper distinguish predictive from causal claims?"
  simplify:
    - "Replace any p < 0.05 claim that lacks a pre-registration with 'exploratory finding'"
    - "Remove effect size interpretations that don't connect to substantive significance"
    - "Cut Bayesian analyses that don't justify the prior"
